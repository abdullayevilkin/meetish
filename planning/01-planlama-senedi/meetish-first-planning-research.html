Meetish: first planning research

2026-10-20 · @ilkin

TL;DR

Meetish solves a real problem: friends who would happily meet don't know who is free right now. Many apps have tried this and most died, because the hard part is getting a whole friend group to post every day, not the technology. Build it as a mobile app that does one thing well ("I'm free / I'm going to X at Y, join me") and launch it one tight community at a time.

The three things that matter most:

  1. Posting in two taps. A plan has to be faster to post than a WhatsApp message, or people won't post.

  2. Density before scale. The app is useless unless 5 to 10 of your real friends use it. Launch in one university, office or friend circle at a time.

  3. Privacy by default. Plans are visible only to people you chose, they expire on their own, and live location is never on by default.

Competitor notes below are from memory and were not looked up for this draft, so treat details as approximate.

1. Is it useful?

Yes, for a specific group: people with a local friend circle and flexible schedules. Think students, young professionals in a city, expats, remote workers and gym or sports groups. It is much less useful for people whose free time is fully planned by family or work.

The problem it solves. Today "who's free for lunch?" means messaging several chats, waiting, and often giving up. Group chats bury plans, and asking directly feels like pressure. A passive "I'm going to X at 13:00, join if you want" removes the awkwardness of asking and the pain of rejection.

Honest risks.

  • Cold start. With 0 to 2 friends on the app it is empty and people leave. This killed most competitors.

  • WhatsApp is "good enough". You must be clearly faster or nicer than posting in a group chat or Instagram Notes.

  • Habit. Plans happen a few times a week, not hourly, so people forget to open the app. Notifications must earn their place.

  • Monetization is hard. Social planning apps rarely charge users. Likely paths are venue partnerships, premium groups or events later.

How to validate cheaply, before writing much code.

  1. Interview 15 to 20 people in your target group: how did they plan their last 3 meetups, and what went wrong?

  2. Run a two-week WhatsApp or Telegram group with 15 to 30 friends where people post only "free now / going to X". Count posts per day and how many lead to a real meetup.

  3. Build a clickable Figma prototype and watch 5 people try to post a plan.

  4. Success signal: at least 1 real meetup per week per active user from the test group, and people asking to invite others.

What works in the market: invite links that open in a browser, tiny friend circles, playful design, and a specific moment (lunch, gym, Friday night). What fails: public feeds, stranger matching without moderation, and needing everyone to install the app before anything works.

4. Extra ideas and open questions

The best ideas below make posting faster or turn a plan into a real meetup; the rest can wait.

Ideas for the core loop

  • Plan templates: one tap for "Lunch", "Gym", "Coffee", "Walk", "Study", "Free now". Learn each user's usual ones.

  • "I'm open to plans" mode: a status for a time window ("free 18:00 to 21:00") without a specific activity, so friends can propose.

  • Join, maybe, or "next time": softer than yes or no, so people still signal interest.

  • Mini chat per plan that disappears after the plan ends.

  • Web invite link: share a plan to WhatsApp or Instagram; friends can join from the browser without installing.

  • Home screen and lock screen widgets showing who is out today.

  • Smart nudges: "3 friends usually have lunch near you on Tuesdays" (only with consent).

Ideas for later

  • Recurring plans ("gym every Mon, Wed, Fri at 19:00") with a rotating crew.

  • Polls to pick a time or place for bigger plans.

  • Calendar sync to show busy or free automatically.

  • Streaks or memories ("you met Aysel 12 times this year"), without being pushy.

  • Venue partnerships: a free coffee for groups of 3 or more who check in.

  • Communities: a running club, a university faculty, a coworking space.

Open questions for you

  • Who is the first user: students, office workers, or a specific friend group you have?

  • Which city or country do you launch in first? This affects language, maps and payments.

  • Only existing friends, or also friends of friends and new people?

  • Is live location part of the product, or just place names?

  • Should a plan be a post (feed) or an invite (sent to specific people)?

  • Do you want to build it yourself, with a team, or with no-code first?

  • How will it make money in year two, if at all?

MVP scope (version 1)

  • Sign up with phone number, profile with name and photo

  • Add friends from contacts or by invite link

  • Create a plan: activity, time or "now", place (free text or pin), audience

  • Feed of friends' plans for today and tomorrow

  • Join or leave a plan; host can cancel

  • Push notification when a close friend posts or someone joins

  • Plans auto-expire; block and report

  • Web link to view and join a plan

Not in MVP: live location, public discovery, strangers, payments, chat beyond a simple comment thread, calendar sync.

6. Design and marketing focus

Position Meetish as the easiest way to turn "free time" into "time with friends", not as another social network or location tracker.

Positioning

  • One line: "See who's free. Join in." or "Your friends' plans, today."

  • Against WhatsApp: no chat noise, plans disappear, you only see what's happening today.

  • Against Snap Map and Life360: no tracking, you share plans, not your location.

Design focus

  • Speed: opening the app to a posted plan in under 5 seconds. The "post" button is the home screen's hero.

  • Today view: a simple timeline of today (morning, lunch, evening) with friends' faces on each plan.

  • Warm and playful, not corporate: big avatars, emoji for activities, light animation when someone joins (Zenly is the reference for tone).

  • Privacy you can see: every plan shows who can see it, in plain words.

  • Low pressure: no read receipts, no "X ignored your plan".

Launch strategy

  1. Start with one dense community you can reach in person: your own friends, a university, a coworking space or a gym.

  2. Seed with 30 to 50 people who already meet often. Ask them to post every plan for 2 weeks.

  3. Expand community by community (next faculty, next office), not city-wide ads.

  4. Use campus or community ambassadors who host the first plans themselves.

  5. Paid ads only after organic retention is proven.

Growth loops

  • Invite loop: every plan shared to WhatsApp or Instagram is a web link; joining asks for a quick sign-up.

  • Content loop: after a meetup, an optional group photo card ("Lunch crew, 4 people") that is fun to post on stories.

  • Contacts loop: "5 of your contacts are on Meetish" at sign-up; notify friends when you join.

  • Community loop: groups for a club or office pull in their members.

Metrics to watch: weekly active users, plans posted per user per week, share of plans that get at least one joiner, and 4-week retention. Aim for at least 30% of plans getting a joiner in the pilot (a guess to test, not a benchmark).
